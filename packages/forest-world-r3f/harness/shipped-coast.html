The coast clip — three shapes, one instrument

The shipped 3D ground ends in the raw hex-union silhouette: the relaxed mesh pins its outer vertices to the hexes it was built from, so an island finishes in 120° corners and reads as a cluster of tiles rather than as one landmass. The approved treatment asks for “grid geometry clipped to a smoothed, gently perturbed coast polygon” — and that coast already exists: smoothCoast() has outset, story-seeded and Chaikin-rounded this boundary all along, and the studio’s 2D map is its only caller. The component was never missing. It was unimported.

none      the map today — the raw hex-union silhouette (CONTROL)
outset    the outset only — bays and headlands, every hex corner kept, 0 new triangles
project   every rim vertex moved ONTO the curve — corners cut, 0 new triangles
subdivide the curve’s own points along each rim edge — the boundary IS the curve

Why three arms and not one. The smoothed coast carries four times the vertices it was handed — this island’s 52 rim vertices become a 208-point curve — so there is no 1:1 displacement of the mesh’s existing boundary to reach for. The three answers give up different things, and only one of them costs triangles.

Every arm shares the ladder, the grain, the relief, the occlusion atlas, the light and the camera. The only thing that moves is which coast the parcels were clipped to.

⚠⚠ The coast this map has always drawn folds — and only a mesh can see it. coast.ts says in terms that perturbing the outset magnitude along the normal means the offset “can never self-intersect”. That is false for a polygon offset in general, and it is false on this island: the outset coast loop crosses itself twice. The studio draws that loop as an SVG fill, and the nonzero fill rule paints a doubled-back region exactly like a single one. A triangulated ground has no such mercy — the two boundary parcels at a crossing overlap, so one capability’s status colour is drawn over ground that belongs to another. Every arm here is therefore capped so no parcel folds, and the caption under each frame reports how many rim vertices the cap bound and how hard. A folded-parcel count above zero is a misreport and the driver refuses the run.

⚠ Read the per-object number, not the frame percentage. A coast is a thin annulus around an island, so “0.4% of the frame changed” reads as nothing at all beside two pictures that are obviously different shapes. Every arm is differenced against none, which counts the coast’s own pixels exactly, and every percentage is quoted against that as well as against the frame.

⚠ The beach’s delivered width in pixels is printed beside every comparison. At the fitted zoom a 7-unit beach is under three pixels wide, so an arm that changed the whole silhouette can come back nearly byte-identical there. A null result has to be readable as a null result rather than as agreement.

The forest rows carry a finding of their own. The coast wave is seeded on the island id, so thirty-five copies of one fixture island wear thirty-five different coasts. The forest stops being a tiled repetition of one shape at the moment the coast lands.
