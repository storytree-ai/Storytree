The trees’ detail — the kit’s texture rung, and how a crown is lit

The owner, on the canopy sheet beside the render he stamped: “this looks nice, but the trees dont seem to have as much detail as our target image.” Two mechanisms, each an arm here over the same dressed ground: the embedded kit shipped one texture rung, 128 texels (decided: the native 2048-texel maps ship, ADR-0508 D1), and every crown is lit by the ground’s derived lights — ambient at the ladder floor, so an unlit needle sits at 80% of a lit one and self-shading has a fifth of the range to happen in. The lighting half is a look: a ladder, rendered here for the owner (ADR-0503 D3), moved on the prop’s material so the ground’s lights and its ladder never change.

What the numbers under each picture are. Pixels moved by more than 20/255 against texture-128 (ADR-0490 D6 — how much of today’s picture an arm changes); the colour-family census and MICRO against the reference; and the crown statistics: over the pixels that differ from bare at all (the props and nothing else, because the ground is byte-identical across arms), the luma at the 10th / 50th / 90th percentile — a flat crown is a narrow spread, a sculpted one a wide one. Payload is in the header: wire bytes, decoded GPU bytes with the mip chain, and the fetch-plus-parse time the shipped canvas pays once at mount.
