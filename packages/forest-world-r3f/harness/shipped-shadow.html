The shadow at forest scale — three remedies, one instrument

The ground occlusion field is allocated over the whole ground’s rect, and its resolution is clamped so that rect fits inside a texture budget. One island spans 234 ground units and gets the authored 3.000 samples per ground unit; a thirty-five-island forest spans about 3,500 and gets 0.585 — 5.1× coarser in each axis, against parcels whose mean diameter is 16.57. The contact pool under a story tree stops being a soft round shadow and goes shrunken and ragged, and it does so precisely when the map becomes the real map.

clamped    the map as it ships — one field over the forest rect, clamped
raised     A — the same field, texture cap lifted until the authored resolution lands
per-island B — one field, one material and one draw call PER ISLAND
atlas      C — ONE field packed over the islands themselves; one material, one draw

Every arm shares the ladder, the grain, the relief, the parcels, the light and the camera. The only thing that moves is how the occlusion field is allocated and how many meshes carry it — so a pixel difference between two arms is the remedy’s and nothing else’s.

⚠⚠ Read the close zoom, not the overview. A contact pool is about 24 ground units across. At the fitted zoom that is a third of a pixel, so all four arms are identical by construction there and a comparison taken at that zoom cannot come back negative — this arc has already produced one pair of byte-identical files that way. The caption under every frame states how wide a pool is in delivered pixels at that zoom, so a null result can be read as a null result rather than as agreement.

The one-island rows are the control. Every remedy must leave a single island pixel-for-pixel as it is today: nothing was wrong there, and a remedy that changed it would be changing the map’s look on the surface everything committed so far was measured on.
