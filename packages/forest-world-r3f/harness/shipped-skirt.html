The stepped skirt — four edges, one instrument

The shipped island’s edge is one flat wall, one storey high: a single quad per rim edge, generated from the parcel above it and wearing that parcel’s health colour, darkened for being near-vertical. The render the owner approved has that edge cut into six rock ledges. This is the sixth and last component of the approved land treatment, and the only one that needed a decision from him before it could be built.

flat          the map today — ONE wall, the parcel’s status colour (CONTROL and DENOMINATOR)
stepped       six ledges, still the status colour — the SHAPE without the rock (his option C)
rock          six ledges, all rock — the approved picture’s cliff (his option A)
soil-over-rock six ledges, the TOP one keeping the status tint (his option B)

⚠⚠ What the owner settled, and how much of it he gave away. Every colour on this map’s ground is a capability’s proof state, and the edge wall inherits the parcel’s tint — so a rock cliff would be the first ground surface that reports nothing. He stamped it on 2026-09-01 and granted more than was asked: “the test should be overall themse color, so we should have flexibility for session to add additional colors as needed, the session should just look at the final and ask itself can I tell what state this island is in.” So the fence moved from composition (every colour must report) to outcome (the state must still be readable) — and applying it is the build’s own job, on these pictures, rather than a question to send back.

⚠⚠ The verdict is not “it renders”. This arc’s standing rule is that every crossing is judged against the approved picture and not against its own best arm — “the image that I stamped as looking awesome was done in isolation and now we trying to do the same with the app constraints in place”. So the approved Cycles render sits at the top of this page and goes through the same instrument as the live frames.

⚠ The reference is an IMAGE, not a scene, and that limits what may be said of it. It is a path tracer’s output at a different resolution, framing and camera. Its anchor / MICRO / STRUCT are comparable to ours on the same axes; its pixels are comparable to nothing. It is measured and never differenced.

⚠ Read the cliff’s own pixel count, not the frame percentage. A cliff is a thin band around an island, so “2% of the frame changed” reads as nothing at all beside two pictures whose edges are obviously different. Every arm is differenced against flat, which counts the cliff’s own pixels exactly.

anchor  the 2nd percentile of luma over the island’s own pixels — its DARKEST value,
        and the quantity the research says the kit’s cliff exists to supply
MICRO   mean |Δluma| between neighbouring pixels — contrast at the pixel scale
STRUCT  std-dev of luma after a 4-px box blur — contrast at the scale an overview still has

Where the numbers come from. The research measured that swapping the kit’s cliff for a competent procedural rock costs 9.8% of structural contrast and lifts the island’s dark anchor by 7.0 luma. Those are measure_land.py’s definitions, re-implemented here so the live frames and the approved render pass through one instrument rather than two that agree today.

Every arm shares the coast clip, the shore fall, the relief, the ladder, the grain, the occlusion atlas, the light and the camera. The only thing that moves is the island’s edge.
