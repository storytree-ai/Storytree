The ground cover — the recipe’s bushes, grass tufts and flowers, at three sizes

The render the owner stamped is not only trees. build_land.py’s scatter() sprinkles 70 undergrowth objects, 120 grass clumps and 26 flowers over the land for every thirteen stands of pines, from the same bought kit, and the research README’s own finding is that the approved island’s colour content is almost entirely its props. This page stands that cover on the shipped map, over the same dressed ground, and ladders one thing: its size.

Why size and not count, measured rather than chosen. The recipe’s counts and sizes were ported literally first (2026-09-03) and the result was invisible: 432 ground-cover props on one island moved 743 px past the 20/255 bar against the canopy, on an island where the canopy itself moved 194,440. The cause is a scale mismatch the port inherited — the recipe’s island is 93.8 ground units across and its pine 4.0 units tall, where this map’s island is 234 and its pine 18, so every signal crossed already scaled and the ground cover crossed literal. Standing more eight-pixel flecks was never going to answer it.

What the numbers under each picture are. Pixels moved by more than 20/255 against canopy (ADR-0490 D6 — how much of today’s picture an arm changes); the object census, with how many of them are ground cover; the widest ground-cover prop actually delivered, in ground units, which is what the criterion marker’s distinctness is argued against (the marker is 4 units and no patch may reach half of it); and the colour-family census and MICRO against the reference. Every dressed arm stands on one ground build, because ground cover casts nothing — and the driver refuses the run if any arm’s caster count disagrees with the canopy arm’s.
