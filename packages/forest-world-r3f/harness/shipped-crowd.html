The shipped ground at forest scale — the adopted ladder on a crowd

The nine-rung shade ladder the map wears since 2026-08-31, beside the four-rung one it replaced, on one island and on thirty-five. Same parcels, same relief, same grain, same occlusion field, same buffer, same camera — the arms differ in the ladder and in nothing else.

Why this page exists, and what it already answered before it drew anything

The increment that asked for it was parked believing the forest map draws many islands, each its own draw call, and that the ladder’s measured per-draw cost would therefore multiply across a real map. That premise is false. ForestWorldCanvas mounts exactly one <CellGround> over the whole descriptor stream, and a territory is an island — so the entire forest’s ground is one merged buffer wearing one material. One draw call, and one ramp upload per frame however many islands are standing. The driver holds the browser’s own renderer.info.render.calls to 1 on the 35-island scene, which is that claim measured rather than read.

So the question changed shape. Three things genuinely do grow with a forest, and the three scenes below separate them:

one           the island every committed figure on this arc was taken on
forest-mono  35 islands, all one status — the extra geometry alone
forest        35 islands in the real forest’s six-status mix — plus the branch spread

Vertex work is submitted whole at every zoom: one mesh has one bounding sphere, so three frustum-culls the forest as a single object and the thirty-four islands off screen are drawn anyway. Status diversity is what forest-mono holds still: the committed single-island figures were taken on an all-healthy island, so every fragment took the same branch of the material’s selection chain. The occlusion field’s resolution is clamped by SHADOW_TEXTURE_MAX; one island gets the full three samples per ground unit, and this is the first thing ever to draw a forest big enough to lose that.

The two timed zooms are the arc’s own — 2 and 8 delivered device px per ground unit — and the buffer is a laptop’s screen rather than the island’s own bounds, so neither holds the whole forest. That is the honest question: what does the rest of the forest cost me while I am looking at one island of it? The overview at the top is fitted, and is never timed.
