The canopy — the healthy island’s grove, and every kit tree’s shadow

The render the owner stamped is forested: thirteen stands of four to eight pines with bare ground between them (build_land.py’s scatter()). The map that drew one pine per capability read sparse beside it, and every one of those pines cast no shadow — the contact pools and cast shadows the ground already carried were drawn under the placeholder story tree alone. That placeholder has since been retired (ADR-0508): the centre pool goes with it, and every shadow on this page is now a bought object’s.

bare        the shipped ground alone — every layer at what ships, nothing standing on it and nothing casting on it (CONTROL and DENOMINATOR)
capability  + one pine per capability, one bloom per signature — NOW casting their shadows
groves-x1  + the healthy island’s grove at the RECIPE’S OWN stand count: 13 stands per recipe-island of area, 4–8 live pines each at 0.55–0.80 of the capability’s height
groves-x2  + the grove at TWICE the recipe’s stands — the SHIPPED pick
groves-x3  + the grove at THREE TIMES the recipe’s stands — the boldest rung rendered

Every arm is the shipped map with one thing changed — what stands on it. The coast clip, the relief, the shore fall, the inset ring, the stepped skirt, the ladder, the grain and the whole ground stack are not re-created here: the page calls shippedGroundBuild, the function the product’s own CellGround calls, handed each arm’s placement list as casters through the same placementCasters the canvas uses. The props are lit through the map’s own pipeline (exact colour, calibrated lights).

The grove grows on green islands and nowhere else. A healthy story’s island is the forested one; a yellow, brown, charred or slate island stays exactly as it drew, and unknown grows nothing. A grove pine is the same green pine at 0.55–0.80 of the capability’s height, never a dead trunk (that is unhealthy’s signal), clear of the 9-unit beach band and of the worn path (the recipe’s own wear < 0.30), and never inside a capability’s own tree — two grove pines may stand at 0.45 of their footprints, because a stand whose crowns touch is what a grove is.

The last three arms are a DENSITY LADDER, not three ideas. The recipe keeps NO clearance between two grove members, so its stands are four to eight trees with crowns freely overlapping; this map does keep one, and its islands are about three times shallower in z, so at the recipe’s own thirteen stands a stand holds about 3.2 pines rather than 4–8. Relaxing the clearance would buy the count back by letting crowns interpenetrate — and the overlap detector would then be measuring against a number chosen to make the placement pass. So the lever is more stands: more clumps rather than tighter ones, every rule still kept. Ship a rung, and a scale-back is GROVE_DENSITY plus SHIPPED_GROVE_ARM — two constants, no re-measurement.

The verdict is the owner’s eye on the forest’s fitted view — the view the map opens on. A same-hue canopy over a same-hue island must leave the island a clean green block, not a smudge. The numbers under each picture are context: pixels moved by more than 20/255 against bare (ADR-0490 D6), the colour-family census, draw calls and triangles. Frame cost is taken from this same page by a second driver (shipped-canopy-cost.mjs, the GPU’s own clock over two independent interleaved sweeps) rather than on it, so a picture and its cost are the same scene.
