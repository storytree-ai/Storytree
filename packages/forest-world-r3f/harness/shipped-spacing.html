The forest's spacing as a fraction of island size (ADR-0521)

The islands were sized from a land-per-capability ratio (ADR-0520) and shrank hard; the 2D map's layout did not shrink with them, and the fitted forest read as dots in a field. The owner decided the fix — “just go straight to C, this really needs to be procedurally determined” — so the 2D row packer's three absolute gaps (40 / 60 / 140 units, by eye) are retired for one ratio: every gap is a fraction of the two islands it separates. The ratio itself is laddered here for his pick.

today the 2D map as it stood before this landing — gaps 40 / 60 / swing 140 units, absolute (CONTROL) spacing-<r> every gap r × the mean radius of the two islands it separates; a lone island swings by its radius + that gap spacing-0 the hex lattice's own floor — the boldest rung; the tiles, not the ratio, set the spacing here

The real forest, not a crowd. Every arm is the studio's own layout of the live corpus at that rung, exported through the map's ?sceneExport=1 bridge and rendered through the shipped 3D pipeline (worldTo3D at the shipped land ratio, dressMapWithCover, shippedGroundBuild). The forest fitted is the view the map opens on: read the land's share of the frame and of its own box. One island at 8 px/unit is the same story on every arm: it is unaffected while its neighbours move. Frame cost is taken by shipped-spacing-cost.mjs on the RTX 2060 and reports (ADR-0517 D4 / ADR-0520 D6).
