Layer 1 — the grass base and its cool/warm hue drift

The approved ground is seven layers, and this is the first: three noise octaves feeding two three-stop grass ramps, chosen between by a slow hue drift. It is the floor the sand, the path, the rock and the grain all composite over, so nothing above it can be judged until it lands. Every constant is transcribed from build_land.py’s mat_attribute() — the script that produced the render the owner stamped — and cited by line.

The gap this arc exists to close. Counting colour families that hold at least half a percent of the island, five bits per channel: the shipped ground has 9, one green holding 27.8% and three shades holding 63.3%. The approved render has 36, none above 5.2%. That is not a green in need of adjustment; it is six absent colour layers.

flat            the map today — status colour + the grain’s normal half (CONTROL and DENOMINATOR)
admissible    grass at 0.005 — the most the reader model admits on the SHIPPED ladder
ladder-limit  grass at 0.20 — the most that leaves any shading depth at all
visible         grass at 0.35 — the least a viewer can actually see

⚠⚠ THE FINDING, AND IT IS WHY THE ARMS ARE THESE ARMS. The three numbers do not overlap. Measured exhaustively over every colour this layer can deliver, against all six shipped ground tokens on all ten ladder rungs:

  fac 0.005  every status still reads as itself — and the layer is invisible
  fac 0.10   the two darkest rungs break: a shadowed building parcel reads as healthy
  fac 0.20   five of ten rungs break; a ladder of 0.88–1.00 is all that survives
  fac 0.35   no ladder survives at all — and this is the first fac you can SEE

The factor needed to see the layer is seventy times the factor at which the map still reports. So the question these pictures put is not which arm looks best. It is what the middle two cost, and whether the fourth has stopped telling the truth.

⚠ What binds is the LADDER, not the palette. The reader holds one reference per status — what lit flat ground looks like, at rung 0.90 — so a rung’s margin is spent by its distance from 0.90 in both directions. The shipped ladder reaches down to 0.77 for the shadow rung, and at 0.77 the proposed/building yellow already sits 0.93 weighted channel units from reading as healthy green before any grass exists. The same wall the grain’s colour half met, at the same place, for the same reason.

⚠⚠ The verdict is not “it renders”, and it is not “pixels changed”. An arm is judged on pixels that move more than 20/255. The touched-pixel count is printed as context and carries no verdict: it scores a 1/255 shift the same as a 164/255 one, and it overstated two increments on this arc roughly fourfold before the owner caught it by eye.

⚠ The reference is an IMAGE, not a scene. It is a path tracer’s output at a different resolution, framing and camera, carrying all seven layers rather than one. Its colour-family count and its MICRO/STRUCT are comparable to ours on the same axes; its pixels are comparable to nothing. It is measured and never differenced.

Every arm is the shipped map with one number changed. The coast clip, the relief, the shore fall, the inset ring, the stepped skirt, the ladder, the grain and the occlusion atlas are not re-created here — the page calls shippedGroundBuild, the same function the product’s own CellGround calls, so the control arm cannot quietly become an older map when a sibling lands.
