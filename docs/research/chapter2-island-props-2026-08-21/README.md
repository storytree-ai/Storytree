## 0. What this found, in one page

**The diagnosis held.** The control island delivers **27 distinct colours** over its whole surface;
the dressed islands deliver **68 to 76** — between 2.5× and 2.8× — with the palette still closed
and **0 off-palette pixels**. The previous round's six islands ranged 27 to 39, which is what a set
separated by lighting looks like in the delivered pixels. Nothing in the shader changed.

**Three things carried nearly all of it, and none of them is a rendering technique:**

1. **A HARD BOUNDARY ON A SMOOTHED COAST.** The island's outline is a cluster of thirteen hexagons
   and it read as a board. Rounding the *rim polyline that props are built along* — not the land —
   and putting a battered stone wall, a band of sand or a swept gravel margin on it changes the
   line the eye traces, which is what a silhouette is. Every direction that reads well has one;
   the one direction that first did not (`shrine`) read as a house on a hexagon until it got one.
2. **A PATH WITH ITS OWN MATERIAL, WIDE ENOUGH TO SEE.** At 7 units it is a faint thread. At 10–11
   it is a walked surface and the eye follows it around the island.
3. **FEWER, BIGGER OBJECTS.** 144 plants at fifteen delivered pixels each are speckle. Thinned to
   18–70% and replaced with hedges, pots, rocks and flowering masses, the same ground reads as
   planting.

**And the thing that did NOT need to change: the renderer.** Not one lever was added to the shader.
The banded material, the ladder, the light and the camera are exactly what they were when the
owner rejected the previous round.

**The tree question got sharper.** On a dressed island the hero tree still accounts for **66% of
all occlusion** and still owns the palette's dark end — but the previous round's finding that
removing it leaves the island "emptier rather than cleaner" no longer holds, because the wall, the
path, the court and the pots survive it. What removal now buys is that the whole plot becomes
visible. See §2.

**What this pass did NOT do: pick a direction.** Five islands, and the honest position is that
`walled`, `hamlet` and `wild` all read as places while `terrace` is the most distinctive and the
least beautiful, and `shrine` is the one that trades count for composition. Which one the art goes
is the owner's, once, on the whole islands (ADR-0392 D1).

## 1. Why this round looks nothing like the last one

The previous round (PR #1480) showed six islands. They differed by **a flag each** — light on,
edge material, ground regional, bevel off. The owner rejected the whole set:

> "these islands dont look nice at all, they all look the same/worse and look as if we havn't
> really broken any new ground. If i look online there are lots of much better looking items,
> what are we missing here, maybe we need better concept art generated by nanobanna for you to
> work of"

**He was right, and the reason is countable.** Counted off his four reference images against ours:

| | distinct kinds of object |
| --- | --- |
| our island | **4** — ground, shrub, flower, tree |
| his pixel-art garden | ~8 — fence, tree, brick planter, flowers, paving, grass, weeds, butterfly |
| his well garden | ~9 — stone wall, paving, well, water, wood fence, potted trees, bushes, flowers, doorway |
| his Japanese pavilion | ~15 — roof, posts, railings, lanterns, pots, bowls, stones, water channel, signposts, crates |
| his cottage | ~15 — house, chimney, porch, greenhouse, fences, paths, hedges, a dozen plant species |

**And the observation that settles which half of the stack was short.** His pixel-art garden has
NO cast shadows, NO ambient occlusion, NO terrain relief and NO bevelled edges — flat coloured
squares — and it reads as a place while ours did not. **Our island already carried more rendering
technique than that picture carries.** So the gap was never a lever missing from the shader. It
was that we drew four kinds of thing where they draw eight to fifteen, and that every reference
has stone AND wood AND brick AND paving AND water where we had exactly one material, matte green,
in slightly different shapes.

He then lifted the fence that had kept every session on rendering:

> "for this experiement props are fine, its meant to show me what's possible so the whole point of
> this experiement was to allow you to just build me a good looking island without worrying about
> it representing the code"

That is now **ADR-0406**, written before any island was drawn: the harness island represents
nothing, so it asserts no state a decoration could misreport, and props, colour and new material
tokens are unfenced on it. **ADR-0406 D2 leaves the product map exactly as it was**, and the
shipped half of `oq-may-the-island-carry-things-that-mean-nothing-and-may-veg` stays open.

## 2. The five directions, and why they differ structurally

A flag can only vary a **quantity**, which is why six flags read as one idea. These five vary what
the island **is**.

| | direction | what it is | buildings? | hero tree? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | **TODAY** | the control — what ships now | no | yes |
| A | **THE WALLED GARDEN** | an enclosed plot: wall, path ring, court, well, pots, fences | a well | yes |
| B | **THE HAMLET** | a place people live: three cottages, worn paths, fenced yards | three cottages | yes |
| C | **THE TERRACES** | worked ground: the capability boundaries become retaining walls | none | **no** |
| D | **THE SHRINE COURT** | a monument, approached: platform, pavilion, gate, lanterns | a pavilion | **no** |
| E | **THE WILD SHORE** | nothing built: sand, rock, a pool, a boat, flowering thickets | **none** | yes |

Two of them deliberately have no buildings and two deliberately have no hero tree, so the set
answers *what is actually carrying this?* rather than only *which do you like?*

- **A vs B** isolates the ENCLOSURE. If both read and only A has a perimeter wall, the wall is not
  what did it.
- **E vs the rest** isolates ARCHITECTURE. E gains five materials and no buildings at all. If it
  reads far better than the rejected round, materials and masses were the gap; if it does not,
  buildings were, and that is a different worklist.
- **D** is the only direction that answers the reference count by SUBTRACTION — few objects, all
  large, on a swept surface.
- **C** is the only direction that could not be built on any other landmass.

### What each one delivers

Measured off the raster by `capture.mjs`, not off the TypeScript that fed it. 14 canvases,
1,201,897 opaque pixels, **88 distinct delivered colours against 285 authored entries, 0
off-palette, 0 foreign-status reads**.

| island | opaque px | **colours** | occluded | luma p2 | p50 | p98 | spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `today` (control) | 85,145 | **27** | 0.0% | 65.5 | 145.1 | 160.9 | 95.4 |
| `walled` | 90,094 | **74** | 15.7% | 65.5 | 135.5 | 175.4 | 109.9 |
| `hamlet` | 88,706 | **76** | 18.4% | 65.5 | 132.7 | 174.7 | 109.2 |
| `terrace` | 80,433 | **68** | 4.7% | 88.8 | 145.1 | 175.4 | 86.6 |
| `shrine` | 79,557 | **35** | 2.8% | 81.0 | 145.1 | 173.0 | 92.0 |
| `wild` | 89,677 | **68** | 15.1% | 65.5 | 135.5 | **189.8** | **124.3** |

**The headline number is the colour count, and it is the diagnosis answered in its own terms.** The
control delivers **27** distinct colours over a whole island. The dressed islands deliver **68 to
76** — between 2.5× and 2.8× — while the palette stays closed and nothing is free-shaded. The
previous round's six islands ranged 27 to 39 and the owner said they all looked the same; that is
what a set separated by lighting looks like in the delivered pixels, and this is what a set
separated by content looks like.

**`wild` reaches a delivered luminance spread of 124.3**, the widest any island in this arc has
produced. The previous round's best was 109.2, and that was the COMPOSED island with every lighting
lever on. The wild shore has no cast shadow advantage over its siblings — it gets there on sand,
which reaches p98 189.8 where the grass tops out around 175.

**`shrine` is the outlier at 35 colours**, and it is the one direction whose thesis is subtraction:
few objects, all large, on a swept surface. It is worth reading as evidence rather than as a
shortfall — if it reads well at 35 colours, the count is not the whole story and composition is.

⚠ **A caution about this table, carried forward from the previous round because it still applies.**
`spread` and `p50` are summary statistics and they are blind to WHERE a colour lands. Read the
pictures; these numbers exist to stop a claim being made that the pictures do not support, not to
replace them.

### The tree, on trial again — now on a dressed island

Same walled garden twice, identical in every other respect:

| | opaque px | colours | occluded | luma p2 | spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tree-with` | 90,094 | 74 | 15.7% | 65.5 | 109.9 |
| `tree-without` | 84,579 | 70 | 5.4% | 82.5 | 92.9 |

The inherited finding survives the dressing and gets sharper: **the hero tree accounts for 66% of
all the occlusion on the island** (15.7% → 5.4%), and it takes the palette's dark end with it — p2

**What is NEW, and it is the reason this pair is worth looking at rather than reading:** the
previous round found that removing the tree left the island *"emptier rather than cleaner"*, because
nothing else was tall or dark. On a walled island that is no longer true — the wall, the path ring,
the court and the pots all survive it, and what the removal now buys is that **the whole plot
becomes visible**, where the tree had been standing on a third of it. This session's own reading,
offered and non-binding: on a dressed island the tree is now competing with the composition rather
than carrying it. That is a look verdict and it is the owner's.

## 3. THE APPEARANCE CALLS, AND WHY — the ADR-0392 D2 / ADR-0398 D3 record

Every appearance decision this pass made, with its reason. An unrecorded art call is a violation
of D2, not an exercise of it. The long form lives beside the code in
`packages/forest-world-r3f/harness/island-dressing.ts`; this is the account.

### The five that govern the whole set

**(a) A DIRECTION IS A PLACE, NOT A SETTING.** Forced by the rejection above: the owner has
already told us that a ladder of settings reads as one idea. So each direction answers "what KIND
of place is this?" and brings its own props, path material, vegetation density and relationship to
the coast.

**(b) THE COAST IS SMOOTHED BEFORE ANYTHING IS BUILT ON IT — the biggest structural change here.**
The island's outline is a cluster of thirteen hexagons and it reads as a BOARD. The previous
round's own write-up named that and could not fix it, because fixing it looked like moving the
land. It is not: two rounds of Chaikin corner-cutting are applied to the **rim polyline that props
are built along**, so a wall, a shore band or a path ring follows a rounded plot while the land
keeps its cells, its parcels and its bevel unchanged. What changes is the line the eye traces,
which is what a silhouette actually is. Two rounds rather than one (one leaves the hexagon corners
legible at delivered size) or three (three starts eating the island's own lobes, and an art choice
that overrode the data's shape would be the wrong kind of improvement).

**(c) VEGETATION IS THINNED, HARD — to between 18% and 70%.** The fixture carries 144 plants, each
about 15 × 12 delivered pixels at 2 px/unit. A hundred and forty-four marks that size do not read
as plants; they read as speckle, which is most of why the rejected round read as textured ground.
Every reference carries between ten and thirty DISCRETE plant masses with actual shapes. The
recovered weight goes into hedges, pots and flowering thickets — bigger objects with silhouettes.
The thinning is a **stable stride**, not a random cull, so the same plants survive across every
island: two directions that differed by which shrubs happened to be drawn would present that
difference as the direction.

**(d) A ROOF IS THE BRIGHTEST THING ON THE ISLAND, AND THAT IS ARITHMETIC.** Measured against the
authored light `norm(-0.45, 0.82, 0.35)` and the four-rung ladder `[0.78, 0.80, 0.90, 1.00]`:

```
top face (0,1,0)                dot=0.821  rung 2 (x0.90)
ANY vertical face               dot<=0.451 rung 0 (x0.78)   <- every orientation
battered side, slope 0.2        dot=0.603  rung 1 (x0.80)
battered side, slope 0.4        dot=0.723  rung 2 (x0.90)
gable roof, ridge//z, 30deg     dot=0.936  rung 3 (x1.00)   <- and its twin slope, 0.486, rung 0
gable roof, ridge//x, 30deg     dot=0.886  rung 2
```

Three consequences, all acted on:
1. **A plain box delivers exactly two colours however it is turned**, so every wall, kerb and step
   is BATTERED at slope ~0.45 — which lifts the light-facing side two rungs while the away side
   stays at the bottom. One number turns a silhouette into a solid.
2. **A wall's top is horizontal, so no lighting choice can separate the coping from the body.**
   Only a second authored token can, hence `stoneLight` over `stone`.
3. **Only a surface pitched toward the light reaches full strength** — the ground's own top faces
   never do. So a pitched roof is simultaneously the brightest and the highest-contrast object
   available, and the roofs that appear are oriented to collect that.

**(e) COLOUR ACCENTS ARE CARRIED BY PROPS AND FLOWERING MASSES, NEVER BY THE GROUND.** ADR-0406 D1
would permit recolouring the ground, and it is deliberately not used. The ground's colour is the
one thing on this island that still resembles a signal on the product map, and a session that
learned "the ground can be any colour" from an experiment would be learning the one lesson that
does not transfer (ADR-0406 D2). Terracotta, blossom, marigold and water are where the colour
goes. **A self-imposed restraint, recorded as one.**

### The seven calls made by LOOKING, after the first render

These are separate from the five above because they were made differently: the five were reasoned
out before anything was drawn, and these seven were made by rendering whole islands at delivered
size and seeing that something did not work. ADR-0392 D2 does not distinguish between the two, and
they are recorded here in the same detail — but the distinction is worth keeping visible, because
every one of these is a case where the arithmetic was right and the picture was still wrong.

**(f) A LINEAR PROP'S HEADING IS NOT A FREE CHOICE, AND THIS ONE COST A WHOLE DIRECTION.** The
shrine court was first flanked by two hedge runs going north–south. At this camera a north–south
ground distance foreshortens by `sin(50°)` while the hedge's own height does not — so a 52-unit
hedge delivered as a narrow, nearly-vertical green **bar** about six pixels wide and forty tall. It
read as two green pillars standing on the island. **An east–west run reads as a hedge and a
north–south one reads as a post**, and that is true of every linear prop here. Replaced with
clipped blocks running east–west, plus set stones.

**(g) A PATH HAS TO BE WIDE ENOUGH THAT THE EYE FOLLOWS IT RATHER THAN NOTICING IT.** Every path
was first authored at 7 units with 2 slabs across, which after the joint gives slabs about 2.5
units — five delivered pixels — and all of them read as faint dotted threads. Widened to 10–11
units and 3 across (22 delivered pixels). Material contrast cannot rescue a track four pixels wide;
**width is the lever**.

**(h) AN ISOLATED MARK UNDER ~10 DELIVERED PIXELS STOPS BEING AN OBJECT.** The shrine's stepping
stones at 4.5 units with a 3.4-unit gap vanished into the grass entirely. At 7 units a single stone
is fourteen pixels and the approach reads as a path of stones. The same floor is why the lanterns
went from 10 units to 13.

**(i) A COMPLETE RING OF FENCE DELIVERS AS A DRAWN ELLIPSE.** The hamlet's yards were closed
eight-sided rings; the projection turns a circle in plan into an ellipse on screen, and an unbroken
uniform one reads as an annotation over the island rather than as a fence on it. Dropping three of
the eight spans opens each yard toward the path — an enclosure with a way in, and no ellipse.

**(j) FIVE RETAINING FRONTS INTERLOCK INTO A MAZE; THREE READ AS TERRACES.** A parcel outline is a
blob, not a contour, and five concentric blobs have no legible order — the walls stopped reading as
terrace edges and started reading as a labyrinth. Three, inset further apart, leave grass between
them, which is what makes each read as the edge of a level.

**(k) THE TERRACES WERE MONOCHROME, AND A TEST CAUGHT IT BEFORE A PICTURE DID.** Built of stone,
gravel, water and hedge alone the direction delivered **five** materials — grey, green and one teal
line — reproducing the exact failure the increment exists to fix, inside a direction meant to fix
it. Two of the five crop rows are now flowering (marigold, blossom), and a rail fence, crates and
pots put wood and fired clay on the hillside. The assertion that caught it (`island-dressing.test.ts`,
a floor of six distinct materials per dressing) is deliberately a floor rather than a target.

**(l) TWO DIRECTIONS MUST NOT CONVERGE, EVEN WHEN EACH IS DEFENSIBLE ALONE.** The shrine's coast
apron was first 9 units wide and 2 slabs across, and at a glance it read like the walled garden's
retaining wall — two different islands arriving at one silhouette. Narrowed to 6 units, 1 across
and half the thickness: still a rounded outline, but visibly FLAT. *"They all look the same"* is the
verdict this increment answers, so convergence is a failure even when the two paths to it are both
sound.

**(m) THE SHRINE'S PAVILION WAS TOO SMALL AND ITS GROUND TOO BARE.** At 28 × 21 on a 40-unit court
the building read as a shed on a beige disc — the court was the object and the building a detail on
it, which is the opposite of what a monument does. Now 40 × 26, and the vegetation went from 18% to
30%: "swept" needs something to be swept clear *of*, and an empty field reads as unfinished rather
than as disciplined.

### The palette

**Eighteen authored material tokens** were added: `stoneLight` `stone` `stoneDark` `paving`
`gravel` `wood` `woodLight` `terracotta` `roofTile` `water` `waterDeep` `sand` `lantern` `doorway`
`hedge` `blossom` `marigold` `thatch`.

**This does not breach ADR-0380 D6 fence 3, and the reading is ADR-0406 D3's.** The fence's
property is that every delivered pixel is an authored `(token × level)` closure entry — which is
what lets `capture.mjs` REFUSE rather than merely report. That property is indifferent to how many
tokens the vocabulary holds; the shadow rung already grew the closure by 39 entries inside it. The
banded shader is untouched. What stays forbidden is unchanged: free continuous shading, gradients,
textures, a nearest-entry snap, or an exception in the checker.

**They are family-less by construction, and NO prop material can deliver a status colour.** Stone
belongs to no status exactly as a flower's petal material belongs to none (ADR-0226 D4), so they
join `familylessTokens()` — without which `capture.mjs` would count every paving pixel as an
unaccounted colour and report a defect that is not one. And `prop-tokens.test.ts` asserts the
harder property: no prop token's delivered colour equals any status family's delivered colour, on
the shadow ladder as well as the lit one. **Measured: 0 collisions.** That test cannot be left to
the capture harness — the harness subtracts family-less tokens *before* asking which family a
colour belongs to, so it is structurally unable to notice this.

Closure sizes: **156 → 228** entries lit, **195 → 285** with the shadow rung. Every pre-prop entry
survives (asserted, not assumed).

## 4. What this costs and what it does not

**The four ADR-0380 D6 fences.** Accessibility stays in the DOM/SVG layer (this is a dev-only
harness page and reaches no product surface). Determinism stays on the scene graph: every dressing
is a pure function of the ground cells, the relief amplitude and a seed — no clock, no random
source — and `island-dressing.test.ts` asserts byte-identical output across separate builds. The
palette holds (section 3). The projection does not move.

**Nothing here is adopted.** All of it lives in `packages/forest-world-r3f/harness/`, which
`sync:web-engine` does not copy (`scope-fence.test.ts`), and ADR-0380 D6 already makes adopting
the live-render experiment a separate event from running it.

**An art module never reads what a parcel MEANS.** The dressings place props by geometry — which
parcel is biggest, which point is furthest from an edge — never by status. That is ADR-0392 D5 and
it is asserted rather than promised: the same dressing built on two scenes differing only in one
capability's status produces identical geometry.

**The one thing direction C would cost, said now rather than at adoption.** It draws capability
boundaries as retaining walls. On a map that asserts proof state, a drawn seam between two
capabilities is exactly the treatment the owner removed on 2026-08-16. Here it asserts nothing
(ADR-0406 D1) — but if C is the direction, that is part of its price, and the arc's end state
obliges saying so.

## 5. Traps carried forward

1. **`bandedColour` RE-QUANTISES its level** onto `SHADE_LEVELS`, so any level that is not a ladder
   member must go through `deliveredColour` instead. Inherited; it silently faked a result once.
2. **`capture.mjs`'s "0 foreign-status reads" is VACUOUS for any darkening**, and is now also
   structurally blind to a prop token colliding with a status colour — see section 3.
3. **`capture.mjs` does NOT exit non-zero on an off-palette pixel.** It prints `PALETTE BREACHED`
   and exits 0. The exit code covers console errors, blank canvases, a duplicate `data-st-tag` and
   the settle timeout — not the palette. Read the report, never the exit code.
4. **`capture.mjs` names SECTION panels by section ORDER.** Inserting a section silently re-points
   every later filename while the run still exits 0. Prefer the tag-named `island-<tag>.png` files.
5. **The island's BOUNDING BOX LIES.** The ground spans 233.8 × 135.1 units, but the corners of
   that box are open water — it is a hex cluster whose outer rows are shorter. A prop line authored
   against the bounds hangs off the edge, so lines are CLIPPED to the coast rather than
   hand-checked against the tile list.
6. **Cell winding is MIXED** (68 positive, 96 negative), so the rim must be chained UNDIRECTED.
   Measured and pinned in `prop-layout.test.ts`: only **49 of the 52** rim vertices have an
   outgoing directed rim edge at all, and the longest directed run strands after **29 of 52**. It
   does not throw — it returns a plausible open polyline, which handed to a fence generator puts a
   wall around half the island and reads as an art choice. Vertex identity must be a key rounded
   to 1e-3; the closest authored vertices are ~1.5 units apart, three orders of magnitude clear.
7. **A parcel loop is not always SIMPLE.** Two of the eleven capabilities (`cap-1`, `cap-5`) are
   two blobs of cells touching at a single corner, so their boundary has one degree-4 vertex and
   the circuit passes through it twice. The intuitive resolution — pair edges *through* the cell —
   closes one blob and silently abandons the other. The rim itself is clean: all 52 vertices are
   degree 2.
8. **Chaikin does not reduce total turning, at all** — 1559.92° before and after any number of
   rounds, exactly, because each corner splits into two whose turns sum to the original. The
   honest smoothness metric is the SHARPEST corner: 60.11° → 30.36° (1 round) → 16.23° (2). Anyone
   reaching for total turning as evidence that smoothing worked will measure nothing.
9. **`addGableRoof`'s slope normal was WRONG when this increment started, and it looked right.**
   A slope running from the eave `(-hx, 0)` to the ridge `(0, rise)` has direction `(hx, rise)`
   and outward normal `(-rise, hx)` — the components SWAP. Written as `(-hx, rise)` it shades a
   roof as if its pitch were `90 − pitch`: exact at 45°, and worse the flatter the roof, so a
   shallow roof shades as a near-vertical wall. At the cottage's 34.8° pitch it delivered dot
   0.839 (rung 2) where the truth is 0.931 — **rung 3**. Since a pitched roof is the only surface
   here that reaches the ladder's top rung, the bug quietly gave away the strongest lever in the
   vocabulary while looking like a deliberately muted roof. Caught by measuring the emitted
   normals through `rungOfNormal`, not by reading the code. Fixed.
10. **`land-definition.ts` contains a deliberate NUL byte**, so `grep` reports it as binary. Use
    `grep -a`. It is authored, not corruption.
11. **Port 5184 may be held by another worktree's harness.** Vite is `strictPort` so it refuses —
   but the existing server still answers with HTTP 200 via SPA fallback, serving a DIFFERENT page.
   Confirm Vite actually bound the port.
