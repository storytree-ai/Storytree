Two things the mocks must not paper over

The waiting state cannot happen today — and the reason got worse since 2026-08-02

Re-measured 2026-08-05: the open-question tier is now completely EMPTY. library artifact list open-question --pg returns 0, and all 20 arcs come back from GET /api/arcs with waiting: false. At the 08-02 mock round there was exactly one live question, oq-public-live-forest-on-the-website, and the finding then was that the tier was unhomed rather than empty — the question existed but carried no arcRef, so under ADR-0267 D4 no arc surfaced it. That question is now gone entirely.

So the trajectory over three days is one question → zero. This is not a rendering gap that a layout can close: the waiting half of this surface has no source of content at all, and it will stay that way until something starts authoring open questions with an arcRef. That makes question 3 below decisive rather than academic — answer it “no” and the waiting column, the waiting lane, and the waiting index rows are permanently decorative.

Every waiting arc below is therefore driven by synthetic questions written for this mock. They are marked wherever they appear, in orange with a dashed border. Nothing dashed-orange is real.

blocked still has no definition, and I have not invented one

ADR-0267 D7 names blocked and deliberately refuses to define it; increment 1 fenced its absence with a test. So the mocks ship a switch, not a predicate. The blocked section puts three candidate meanings side by side and lets you flip between them and watch the real 20 arcs re-sort. Whichever you pick becomes the definition; picking none is also a result.

And one thing deliberately not drawn: a progress bar

ADR-0267's Context says outright that “a percentage-complete bar would answer none of that”, and an arc has no denominator — endState is prose, not a checklist. So “what step are they up to” is rendered as a three-part position read, never a fraction:

distance — one pip per landed increment
4d ago momentum — days since the last landing, the number that exposes a stall
1 ready plan next — a ready plan, a proposed ADR, or nothing queued

Two requirements that postdate the 08-02 round — none of the four layouts answers either

1 · Parked work is now the biggest thing on an arc, and no mock draws it. ADR-0298 D1 replaced the retired proposal kind with parked entries stored on the arc itself. Measured today: 40 parked entries across 12 of the 20 active arcs — verification-integrity-arc carries 12, cli-write-fidelity-arc and session-decoupling-arc 7 each. The 08-02 extract had no proposals field at all, so every layout above renders “next” as a ready plan, a proposed ADR, or nothing queued — and answers “nothing queued” for arcs that in fact have a dozen items parked on them. That is the single most misleading thing on this page right now. Whichever layout you pick needs somewhere for parked work to live.

2 · The surface must carry a factory-floor health signal, not only per-arc state. Your direction on 2026-08-04, parked on this arc as factory-floor-health-signal: “I think this will be solved when we setup the arc tracker/dashboard so when this stuff needs my attention we can make it very visible that there is something wrong on the factory floor.” Every one of the four blocked candidates answers what is the state of THIS arc. None answers is the floor healthy — the same bottleneck hit eight times in a week lights up no arc state at all. The signal already exists in the data (friction reinforcement counts), so this is thresholding and rendering, not new instrumentation — but it needs a home in the chosen layout, and retrofitting it after the layout ships is the expensive order.

Both are folded into question 1 below rather than mocked separately: the 08-02 mock round is done and re-mocking is not what this session is for.

the undefined state

What should blocked mean?

Three candidates computable from what is already stored, plus one that is not. The table shows exactly which real arcs each would light up today — that is the whole argument.

Apply to the mocks above: no blocked predicate — the ADR-0267 default

B4 — “the arc is waiting on you”

The meaning you may actually want, and the only one that cannot be computed today. Around five of the twenty are sitting on an owner look, an owner pick, or a spend approval — but that fact lives in prose inside the last increment's outcome, not in any field.

Cost: one authored value on the arc, written by the session at the moment it halts. Cheap to add, but it is new authored state, which is why I am not slipping it in as a derived predicate.

Why waiting and blocked must stay apart

D7 forbids collapsing them, and the data shows why it would be tempting. Under B1, verification-integrity-arc becomes blocked because three ADRs are proposed — and you can watch it leave the waiting column when you press B1 above. Under B4 it would not be blocked at all: it landed a PR yesterday and is moving.

waiting = the arc needs an answer from you. blocked = the arc cannot proceed. An arc can be neither, either, or both — which is an argument for showing them as two marks rather than one column.
