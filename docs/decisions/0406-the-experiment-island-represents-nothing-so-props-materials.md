# ADR-0406: The experiment island represents nothing, so props, materials and colour are unfenced on it

## Status

accepted (2026-08-21) — decided/directed by the owner in conversation on 2026-08-21. Design-time alignment IS the ratification (ADR-0110); no second end-of-flow ask.

**Amends** ADR-0367 — D5's rule ("the land's status tint survives, and it outranks the art") is
scoped to art that REPRESENTS WORK. It is untouched for the product map. It does not bite on the
`packages/forest-world-r3f/harness/` experiment surface, because that surface asserts no proof state
for an art choice to misreport.

## Context

`chapter2-island-that-looks-good-first-arc` inverts its predecessor's order: make islands that look
good first, then work backwards to what is required to hold them up. Its first attempt (PR #1480)
showed the owner six whole islands. He rejected the whole set, verbatim:

> "these islands dont look nice at all, they all look the same/worse and look as if we havn't really
> broken any new ground. If i look online there are lots of much better looking items, what are we
> missing here, maybe we need better concept art generated by nanobanna for you to work of"

**The diagnosis that followed is the reason this ADR exists, and it is not a rendering diagnosis.**
Counted off the owner's four reference images against our island:

| | distinct kinds of object |
| --- | --- |
| our island | **4** — ground, shrub, flower, tree |
| his pixel-art garden | ~8 — fence, tree, brick planter, flowers, paving, grass, weeds, butterfly |
| his well garden | ~9 — stone wall, paving, well, water, wood fence, potted trees, bushes, flowers, doorway |
| his Japanese pavilion | ~15 — roof, posts, railings, lanterns, pots, bowls, stones, water channel, signposts, crates |
| his cottage | ~15 — house, chimney, porch, greenhouse, fences, paths, hedges, a dozen plant species |

**The observation that settles which half of the stack is short.** His pixel-art garden has NO cast
shadows, NO ambient occlusion, NO terrain relief and NO bevelled edges — flat coloured squares — and
it reads as a place while ours does not. Our island already carries MORE rendering technique than
that picture carries. So the gap is not a lever we forgot to build in the shader; it is that we draw
four kinds of thing and they draw eight to fifteen, and that every reference has stone AND wood AND
brick AND paving AND water where we have exactly one material, matte green, in slightly different
shapes.

**Why the arc could not close that gap on its own authority until now.** Everything in the missing
list is a DECORATIVE object or an off-status colour, and both were fenced:

- **ADR-0367 D5** — the land's status tint outranks the art; a treatment that gives up per-cell
  status colour is refused without a further owner decision, "because the tint is semantic state and
  this ADR is about appearance."
- **ADR-0226** — the living-surface vocabulary: what each drawn thing MEANS. A plant's colour is its
  capability's status; a flower's form is its UAT verdict.
- **ADR-0392 D5 / ADR-0398 D7** — an art call may never settle a SEMANTIC question under cover of
  appearance. So a session could not simply decide that props were allowed.

That is a correct fence and it was doing real work — on a map. It also meant that every session on
this track spent its increment on the only thing it was permitted to move, which was rendering
technique, which is precisely the half that the reference count says is not short. The open question
`oq-may-the-island-carry-things-that-mean-nothing-and-may-veg` was authored to put the fork in front
of the owner. He answered its experiment half on 2026-08-21, verbatim:

> "for this experiement props are fine, its meant to show me what's possible so the whole point of
> this experiement was to allow you to just build me a good looking island without worrying about it
> representing the code"

**The licence therefore exists, and it currently lives in an increment body and a corrected open
question — which is durable, but is not where a session looks when it is deciding whether it is
allowed to draw a fence.** This ADR is that record. It is written FIRST, before any island, because
the failure it prevents is a later session re-deriving the fence from ADR-0367 D5 and spending
another increment on shadow depth.

## Decision

**D1 — THE EXPERIMENT ISLAND REPRESENTS NOTHING, AND THAT IS WHAT UNFENCES IT.** The islands rendered
by `packages/forest-world-r3f/harness/` are pictures, not maps. They assert no capability's proof
state, no UAT verdict and no parcel boundary that anything depends on. Because there is no state
being asserted, there is no state that a decorative object or an off-status colour can misreport, and
ADR-0367 D5 has nothing to bite on. Concretely, on that surface and with no further permission:

- **PROPS** — fences, walls, retaining edges, paths and paving, rocks, pots and planters, lanterns,
  steps, bridges, water, and small structures.
- **COLOUR** — plants may be any colour; the ground may be any colour; nothing on the island has to
  encode a status.
- **NEW AUTHORED TOKENS** for prop materials — stone, wood, brick, terracotta, water, tile and
  whatever else the props need.

A session on this arc does not ask again, and does not re-derive the question from ADR-0367.

**D2 — THE PRODUCT MAP IS UNTOUCHED, AND THIS IS THE HALF THAT MATTERS MOST.** Nothing here licenses
a prop or an off-status colour on the island THE APP DRAWS. ADR-0367 D5 and ADR-0226 are in force
there exactly as written. On a surface that asserts proof state a decorative object competes for the
reader's attention with objects that mean something, and an off-status plant colour either asserts a
state we do not hold or makes status unreadable — both are the ADR-0367 D5 failure. The shipped half
of `oq-may-the-island-carry-things-that-mean-nothing-and-may-veg` stays OPEN and returns in full
force the moment any of this is proposed for adoption. Adoption is already a separate event from
running the experiment (ADR-0380 D6), so there is no path by which D1 reaches the product without
someone answering that question first.

**D3 — THE LOCKED PALETTE IS A VOCABULARY FENCE, NOT A SIZE FENCE, AND PROPS GROW THE VOCABULARY.**
ADR-0380 D6 fence 3 requires that a live render stay BANDED to an authored palette rather than
shipping as a generic 3D render. The property that carries the fence is that **every delivered pixel
is an entry in a finite, enumerable, AUTHORED closure**, which is what lets `capture.mjs` REFUSE
rather than merely report. That property is indifferent to how many tokens the vocabulary holds —
the shadow increment already grew it by 39 entries inside this fence. So a prop material is added by
**authoring its token**, keeping the banded shader, and keeping the capture green. Free continuous
shading, gradients, textures, a nearest-entry snap, or an "ignore these pixels" exception in the
checker are all still refused: an exception is how a closed palette stops being closed.

⚠ **THE PROPERTY IS ENUMERABLE AUTHORSHIP, NOT THE MULTIPLICATION** — corrected in place on
2026-08-22 (ADR-0139: the decision did not change, its description had been overtaken). This clause
originally read "an authored `(token x level)` closure entry", which named the arithmetic that
happened to compute an entry at the time rather than the property being fenced. That reading would
forbid a legitimate move for the wrong reason, which is the exact failure this ADR was written
FIRST to prevent. The move it would have forbidden, and which is now taken on the experiment
surface: a token may declare a **shade key** and compute its rung entries as a mix between two
authored colours instead of as `token x level`, so that a shaded face can ROTATE IN HUE rather than
only darken. It is not a loosening — a keyed token still delivers exactly one colour per ladder
rung, the ramp is still enumerated by `tokenRamp` / `shadowRamp`, `landPalette` still closes over
it, and the capture still refuses an off-palette pixel. What is fenced is the closed, authored,
refusable set; how an entry inside it is computed is not.

The measurement that forced the correction, and the reason it could not be reached by deepening the
shadow: in the owner's named reference a shaded face has rotated 22 to 61 degrees of hue at roughly
0.6x its value, and a multiply by one scalar leaves hue and saturation exactly where they were, so
the old form **could not express the reference's shading at all**
(`docs/research/chapter2-islanders-canopy-2026-08-22/`). Shade keys are scoped to family-less prop
tokens; keying a STATUS family would change what the land's colour asserts and is a SEMANTIC
question ADR-0392 D5 / ADR-0398 D7 put beyond an art call — it is priced in that research artefact
and deliberately not decided.

**D4 — PROP TOKENS ARE FAMILY-LESS BY CONSTRUCTION, AND MUST BE DECLARED SO.** `statusFamilyOf`
answers "which status does this delivered colour belong to". A stone or water token belongs to none,
exactly as the UAT flowers' materials already belong to none (ADR-0226 D4). Prop tokens therefore
join `familylessTokens()` alongside the flower materials and the shared overrides. This is not
bookkeeping: without it a foreign-status audit reports every paving pixel as an unaccounted colour and
reads as a defect, which is the instrument-fails-for-the-wrong-reason failure this track has paid for
repeatedly. **A prop token must never deliver a colour a status family can already deliver** — that
would make an ornament indistinguishable from a status read, which is the one thing this decision must
not do even on a surface that asserts nothing, because the island is judged by a human who will carry
what he learns to the map.

**D5 — WHAT IS UNFENCED IS THE ISLAND'S CONTENT, NOT THE ARC'S RULES.** The three other ADR-0380 D6
fences do not move: accessibility stays in the DOM/SVG layer over any canvas, determinism stays on the
scene graph (a prop's placement is a pure function of the scene and its options, seeded, never a live
random), and the projection does not move — 2.5D isometric at the declared camera. ADR-0392 D1 and
ADR-0398 D2 do not move either: the owner's look is taken ONCE, at the END, on WHOLE islands at
DELIVERED size. And ADR-0392 D2 / ADR-0398 D3 bind HARDER as this authority widens — every appearance
call is the driving session's and every one is RECORDED WITH ITS REASON. An unrecorded art call is a
violation of that decision, not an exercise of it.

## Consequences

**The good.** The arc can finally spend an increment on the half of the stack the evidence says is
short. A session reading ADR-0367 D5 tomorrow finds the scope it actually has rather than inferring a
fence that was lifted, which is the specific cost this ADR was written first to avoid — the previous
round spent its whole increment on rendering levers because that was the only ground it believed was
open to it. And the product map keeps its guarantee unweakened, stated in one place, with the
still-open half named.

**The cost, stated plainly.** The experiment and the product now diverge, deliberately, and the
divergence is the point. Whatever the owner likes about a props-and-materials island is not
automatically available on the map — some of it may be available only by answering the shipped half of
the open question, and some may never be available at all. The honest consequence is that the
direction he picks may cost a semantic decision he has not yet made, and the arc's end state already
obliges writing down what the chosen direction costs. That is where this lands rather than being
discovered later.

**The risk that needs watching, and it is a human one rather than a mechanical one.** An experiment
island full of walls, water and lanterns sets an expectation. When the map cannot carry those things,
the map will look worse by comparison to a picture nobody said it had to match. The defence is D2's
plainness and the arc's obligation to price the direction — not a quieter experiment, because a
quieter experiment is exactly what produced six islands the owner rejected for looking the same.

**A second risk, smaller and mechanical.** A growing token vocabulary makes the palette check pass
more easily by construction — every colour is authored, so authoring more colours is the way to make
anything legal. The check therefore proves less about restraint than it did. It still proves the
property it was built for (no free continuous shading, no blends, no snapped colours), and D4's
never-collide rule is what keeps the growth honest where it matters. If the vocabulary ever grows to
the point where "banded" stops meaning anything, that is a finding to ESCALATE rather than a reason to
quietly abandon the banded material.

**What does NOT change.** The gate, the proof discipline, ADR-0226 on the product, ADR-0367 D5 on the
product, the four ADR-0380 D6 fences, ADR-0392 D1/D2, ADR-0398, and the separation between running the
live-render experiment and adopting it.
