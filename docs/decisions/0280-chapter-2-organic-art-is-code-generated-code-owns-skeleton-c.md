# ADR-0280: Chapter 2 organic art is code-generated: code owns skeleton, camera and growth; models supply components

## Status

accepted (2026-08-01) — decided/directed by the owner in conversation on 2026-08-01. Design-time alignment IS the ratification (ADR-0110); no second end-of-flow ask.

**Amends [ADR-0219](0219-generative-image-models-enter-the-art-pipeline-author-time-o.md).**
Its author-time-only boundary, committed-assets rule and no-runtime-call posture stand unchanged and
are strengthened here. The narrow amendment is the model's ROLE: a generative image model is a
supplier of individual component assets and textures that our code then orchestrates, not the author
of a whole frame or of a growth track.

**Amends [ADR-0274](0274-pixellab-animates-organic-growth-over-the-app-owned-svg-isla.md).**
D1 (the app-owned SVG island is the sole land substrate), D3 (the generated asset fits the app's
camera; the camera does not move to fit the asset) and its runtime safeguards stand without change.
D2's reservation of organic growth tracks to PixelLab is widened to any author-time source, code
first. ADR-0274 is not superseded and its arc is not closed; that track remains live and unselected.

## Context

Chapter 2's hero tree is unresolved after four rounds. Rounds 1–3 were generative-model tracks
(PixelLab, with a Nano Banana comparison). Round 3 ranked eight of them and left five named problems
open; two of those are now settled facts rather than open questions:

**The camera is a blocker and PixelLab cannot meet it.** All eight round-3 tracks were front
elevation against a low top-down plate. The owner directed on 2026-08-01 that this must be fixed
before any owner LOOK. An 11-generation camera-projection probe (arc increment #1062) then
established that PixelLab will not obey a camera *word* — the `view` parameter with isometric and
aerial vocabulary, and in-context `create_map_object`, all return side elevation or bare terrain. A
generation top-up would not have fixed it. The remaining workaround needs roughly 40 generations and
18 remain, so it is also budget-blocked.

**Connectedness and growth were never had together.** Round 3 §5 item 4: three of the eight tracks
won per-frame connectedness by freezing the tree, which is the "slowly revealed static image" family
the owner ranked last. Round 3 concluded it had established each property alone and neither together.

Round 4 tested whether art produced entirely by our own code could do better. Four model-free
generators were built; the workflow died before its writeup, so three were rendered afterwards from
the committed generators at zero cost. `docs/research/chapter2-code-only-art-2026-08-01/VERDICT.md`
records the result and its gaps.

Three findings drive this decision:

1. **The finish, not the method, made procedural art look cheap.** Two near-identical procedural
   skeletons were emitted through different renderers. Through SVG it reads as vector clipart;
   through a pixel rasteriser it is competitive with exp-16, the track the owner called his
   favourite. This is a controlled result, and it is the direct re-test of ADR-0264's reversal —
   that decision's rig was rendered through layered SVG, and its reversal may have generalised from
   "SVG procedural looks schematic" to "procedural looks schematic".
2. **The camera is a free scalar in code.** `camera_elevation_deg: 20.0`, with the ground plane
   compressed to `sin 20° = 0.342` calibrated against `forest-world`'s own shadow ellipse. The
   blocker that is structurally unavailable in the model costs one re-run here.
3. **Quality and camera are in different experiments.** The best-looking code track has no projection
   at all; the track with the calibrated camera has the weakest surface. Combining them is the work,
   and it is unproven.

The owner is not committed to any vendor, considers the PixelLab budget spent, and would use Nano
Banana or another vendor where a component asset is genuinely wanted. The stated goal of this
initiative is **to see the ceiling of code-generated art**, in order to assess whether reopening 3D
is warranted.

## Decision

**Chapter 2's organic art is generated by our own code. Code owns the skeleton, the camera and the
growth; a generative model may supply individual component assets and textures that the code
orchestrates.**

### D1 — Code owns skeleton, camera and growth

The tree's topology, its growth over time and its projection are computed by our code, from a seed,
deterministically. Specifically:

- **Topology is continuous by construction.** The tree at any age is a strict prefix of the mature
  tree, or an equivalent construction in which a new branch order emerges from zero extent rather
  than appearing. Randomness is keyed on a part's identity, never on a draw counter, so adding a
  branch cannot reshuffle the tree. Freezing the tree to buy per-frame connectedness is forbidden —
  that is the rejected static-reveal family.
- **The camera is a declared parameter**, calibrated to the app's established isometric plate rather
  than chosen by taste, and recorded in the track's registration metadata.
- **Growth pacing is authored**, not an accident of the sampling — a track whose change is crowded
  into its last frames is rejected or re-timed.

### D2 — The chosen technique is the hybrid: code skeleton and camera, raster finish

The direction is `code-your-own-call`'s skeleton, girth and calibrated camera put through
`code-pixel-rasteriser`'s shading, quantisation and selective-outline finish. This generalises the
lesson of exp-18 — the owner's second-favourite round-3 technique, in which pure code already owned
the skeleton and a model owned only the skin — with the skin also moved into code.

**Vector output is not the finish.** SVG emission is retained only as a debugging or structural view.
The delivered asset is raster.

### D2a — Blender is admitted as an author-time render backend behind the same seam

Blender is adopted as a **third finish backend**, alongside the pixel rasteriser and the SVG
structural view. It is free, GPL, and fully scriptable, and it is admitted **because this arc's job is
to find the ceiling** — the one 3D data point we hold (`code-sdf-volume`) is a sphere-tracer we
hand-rolled in numpy inside a single workflow phase, so it measures the ceiling of what we can
hand-roll, not the ceiling of 3D.

- **Code still owns skeleton, camera and growth (D1 is unchanged).** Blender is driven headless from
  our own script — `blender --background --python <script>` — which generates the geometry, sets the
  camera and steps the growth. Blender occupies the *finish* slot, exactly where the pixel rasteriser
  sits. It is a renderer, never an authority, and no `.blend` file is a source of truth: the script
  is.
- **It does not replace the pixel rasteriser.** That track is the best-looking code result to date and
  stays a live candidate. The backends are compared, not ranked by adoption order.
- **The raster back half still applies.** A Blender frame that ships as a generic 3D render is the
  ADR-0145 failure reproduced. Output passes through the same quantisation, palette snap and
  selective-outline treatment that keeps a frame inside the island's idiom.
- **Author-time only, exactly as for every other vendor (ADR-0219).** Blender never enters the
  runtime, the build or the deployed artifact, and is not a workspace dependency. It is an operator
  tool on the authoring machine; the committed PNG frames and the generating script are what the repo
  carries.
- **Determinism is constrained, not assumed.** Renders use **CPU Cycles** with a fixed seed and a
  fixed sample count, against a **pinned LTS Blender** — **5.2.0 LTS**, which is the version installed
  and used for the first spike. EEVEE and GPU rendering are not stable across drivers and machines and
  are not used for delivered frames. Where exact byte-reproducibility cannot be shown, ADR-0219's
  existing rule governs: the **committed frame is the source of truth**, not the renderer.

`bpy` from PyPI is not a usable route here — no wheel exists for the machine's Python 3.14.5 — and the
headless-application route is preferred regardless, since Blender bundles its own interpreter and can
be version-pinned.

**The scope objection is noted and remains the owner's to weigh.** D5 records that the live argument
against 3D is over-engineering a non-core surface, and a Blender pipeline is heavier than a
single-file generator. It is admitted here because it is free, author-time only, and directly serves
the ceiling question this arc exists to answer — not because heavier tooling is assumed better.

### D3 — Generative models are demoted to component and texture suppliers

A generative image model may author **individual component assets and textures** — a leaf blade, a
bark texture, a flower, a lobe motif — which our code then places, lights, composes and animates. It
does **not** author a whole frame, a growth track, a pose sequence or a silhouette.

This is vendor-agnostic. The PixelLab budget is treated as spent; **Nano Banana is the default source
where a component is wanted**, and any vendor may be substituted. ADR-0219's author-time-only
boundary is unchanged and absolute: no vendor request, credential, model call or generated clock
enters the runtime, the build or the deployed environment. Assets are committed locally with
provenance.

### D4 — The arc's purpose is a ceiling demonstration

This initiative exists to show the owner **how good code-generated art can get**, so that the 3D
question can be assessed on evidence. It is not gated on shipping a selected hero tree, and it does
not close or de-prioritise the PixelLab arc, which stays active with its own comparison lab awaiting
an owner LOOK.

### D5 — 3D: the record is corrected, and it stays closed on SCOPE, not on identity

The earlier 3D rejection ([ADR-0145](0145-act-2-walks-the-real-2-5d-map-the-r3f-forest-retreats-to-far.md))
reads, in the owner's words at the attestation gate, *"it looks ugly and doesnt represent story
tree"*. That has been read since as a rejection on product identity. **The owner corrected this on
2026-08-01: the objection was essentially that the output was ugly.** Cosy top-down 3D games are
aesthetically aligned with storytree, and the owner has seen ones he likes.

The live argument against 3D is therefore **not** identity and **not** the look ceiling. It is
**scope discipline**: storytree gamifies software engineering, so the agent harness is the core
product. The look should be nice, but this is a gamified agent harness and not a game, and a 3D
pipeline risks over-engineering a non-core surface.

Consequently:

- **3D at runtime stays closed**, as decided by ADR-0145, ADR-0214 ("Rejected: a three.js / WebGL
  substrate swap") and ADR-0069 D3.
- **Author-time 3D is not rejected on identity grounds and has not yet been tested on its merits.**
  ADR-0069 D4's rejection of WebGL is grounded in the requirement for pixel-stable deterministic
  output, which a CPU-side renderer satisfies; ADR-0219 already contemplates an author-time 3D
  maquette. The one round-4 attempt (`code-sdf-volume`) failed to resolve into a tree and is
  undiagnosed — and it is **weak evidence about 3D**, because it is a sphere-tracer we hand-rolled in
  numpy in a single workflow phase rather than a production renderer.
  **D2a now supplies the instrument** that makes this question answerable: headless Blender, CPU
  Cycles, driven by our own script. Author-time 3D moves from "untested hypothetical" to "testable
  within this arc", which is precisely the evidence the owner needs to weigh the scope call below.
- **Reopening 3D is an owner decision, taken against the ceiling this arc demonstrates**, and it is
  to be argued on scope — is this worth building for a non-core surface — rather than re-litigating
  the look.

### D6 — Unchanged

The shared app owns semantic state, normalized progress, frame selection, timing, easing, holds,
Next, Back, Replay, reduced-motion settlement, sockets and painter order. The existing app-owned SVG
island, coast and established camera remain the sole land substrate (ADR-0274 D1). Any generated or
computed asset is appearance only. If a code or 3D direction would change the land substrate, that is
a fork raised to the owner, not an assumption.

## Consequences

**Good.**

- The camera blocker the owner named becomes a declared scalar instead of a vendor limitation that a
  paid top-up could not fix.
- Topology continuity is guaranteed by construction rather than bought by freezing the tree, closing
  round 3's central unresolved question.
- Iteration is free and deterministic: no per-frame generation cost, no budget gate, byte-identical
  re-runs, so art direction becomes a parameter sweep rather than a purchase.
- The decision log stops carrying an over-generalised reading of ADR-0264's reversal ("procedural
  looks cheap") that the round-4 control experiment refutes.
- The 3D question is reframed onto the axis the owner actually holds — scope — instead of a look
  verdict that has been superseded by better tooling.

**Costs / risks.**

- **The hybrid is unproven.** Quality and camera live in different experiments; joining them is real
  work and may reveal that the rasteriser's finish depends on the front elevation it was tuned for.
- **Palette is borrowed, not directed.** Three code tracks subset exp-16's colours. An independent
  art direction against the island plate has not been attempted and may be where code is weakest.
- **No code track has been composited on the real island**, which is where round-3 tracks lost points
  won on transparency. Nothing here is owner-attested.
- Demoting models to component suppliers gives up whole-frame art direction, which is the thing
  PixelLab was actually good at — exp-16's style match remains the best in the pool.
- A ceiling demonstration can consume real effort and end in "not good enough", which is an accepted
  outcome of this arc rather than a failure of it.
- `code-sdf-volume`'s failure is undiagnosed, so the author-time-3D option is being held open on an
  experiment that did not work — mitigated but not removed by D2a, which replaces the hand-rolled
  renderer with a production one.
- **D2a adds a ~1 GB author-time tool to an initiative whose live objection is over-engineering.** It
  is free, author-time only and absent from the runtime, build and workspace dependencies, but it is
  still a heavier pipeline than a single-file generator, and it must earn its place against the pixel
  rasteriser on results rather than on capability.
- A production renderer makes it *easier* to ship a frame that looks like generic 3D — the exact
  appearance ADR-0145 rejected. The raster back half is what prevents that, and it is now
  load-bearing rather than stylistic.
